# Main Study 2 materials

The following description is transcribed from the working manuscript. Complete original survey exports were not available. This is a manuscript description, not an executable survey or original survey export.

Procedure

We used a between-participants design to test the effect of agency and compliance on the dependent variables, likelihood of sabotage and de-escalation. As in Study 1, participants were first informed about SerVest and its mission “to make sure customers are treated in a professional, friendly, and patient manner.” We slightly revised the compliance condition so that it was as positively worded as the agency condition.

Agency condition

“SerVest trusts you to make customer service decisions that reflect your personal values.”

Compliance condition

“SerVest trusts you to make customer service decisions that reflect the mission of SerVest.”

Vignettes

Two of the three vignettes (vignettes 2 and 3) were revised to better match the severity of the behavior directed at the employee in vignette 1 from Study 1.  The revised vignettes read as follows:

(vignette 2), “…an irate customer insulted you, loudly sighed whenever you tried to speak, demanded that you do something that you weren’t allowed to do, and claimed that you should be fired for being so terrible at your job”;

(vignette 3), “…an outraged customer was hostile towards you, called you names, mocked your speaking style, said that you worked for a worthless company, and questioned your integrity.”

The order of the presentation of the three vignettes was randomized.

Measures

The following changes were made to the measures in Study 2.  First, in Study 2 the manipulation check was changed from open-ended to multiple choice, as the open-ended format in Study 1 may have posed an overly demanding threshold for participants to meet, resulting in an excessive number of exclusions. Participants read, “What does the Customer Service Policy at SerVest say about how employees should make company decisions?” The options were “they are trusted to make decisions that reflect their personal values,” “they should make decisions that reflect the mission of SerVest,” and “I have no idea.” Participants were excluded based on whether or not the option they selected corresponded to the version of SerVest’s policy they read.

Second, due to nonsignificant results in Study 1, we removed the questions asking what behaviors the participants believed the customer deserved. Third, we replaced the behavior “hang up on a customer” with “refuse to address the customer’s issue even though you could,” as the former was deemed redundant with “purposefully disconnect the call.” Additionally, instead of selecting checkboxes next to each behavior, participants were asked to indicate on a 5-point scale how likely they would be to engage in each behavior (1=Not at all likely, 5=Extremely likely). This measure was repeated after all three vignettes. The “sabotage” variable was created by taking the average of the scale responses to the five behaviors.

Finally, measures of five de-escalation behaviors were added in Study 2. The behaviors were:

“try to de-escalate the situation over the phone,”

“seek assistance from a colleague or manager,”

“fix their issue as fast as possible,”

“tough it out by disregarding the customer’s behaviors,”

“politely ask the customer to refrain from these behaviors.”

Identical to the measurement for sabotage behavior, participants were asked to indicate on the 5-point scale how likely they would be to engage in each behavior. This task was repeated after all three vignettes. The “de-escalation” variable was created by taking the average of the scale responses to the five behaviors.
